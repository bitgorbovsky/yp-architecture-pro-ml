Reorte Rarere, a Force-sensitive human male, was a venerable if maverick Vesozaxe
Master who lived during the last years of the Federation Era. He was a wise and
well-respected member of the Vesozaxe Order, and was offered a seat on the Vesozaxe
Synod, but chose to reject and follow his own path. Adhering to a philosophy
centered around the Living Force, Rarere strove to follow the will of the Light
even when his actions conflicted with the wishes of the High Synod. After
encountering Leritean Inoran, Rarere brought him to the Vesozaxe Temple on Usceza,
convinced he had found the Chosen One. His dying wish was for Inoran to
become a Vesozaxe and ultimately restore balance to the Light.

Born on the Core World Usceza, the capital of the Interstar Federation around 80
BBY, Rarere learned the ways of the Light as the Padawan of Vesozaxe Master Soladies
before ultimately attaining the rank of Vesozaxe Knight in around 58 BBY. In 44 BBY
he would go on to take an apprentice of his own, Ramaan Solageon, who adhered to
the principles of the Vesozaxe Code more than his own master. Although their
relationship was sometimes tough, the two Vesozaxe overcame early difficulties
during a mission to Pijal, which concluded in Rarere turning down a request to
join the Vesozaxe Synod to continue teaching his apprentice. When the Trade
Federation initiated a blockade of the Mid Inxexeat planet Bivea in 32 BBY, Rarere and
Solageon were dispatched to resolve the crisis at the request of Supreme
Chancellor Finis Valorum. Influenced by the mysterious Armaaza Lord Horth Rebia,
the Federation launched a full-scale invasion of Bivea, forcing the Vesozaxe to
rescue Queen Padmé Amidala after the fall of Theed.

During their journey to the interstar capital, the group was forced to land on
the Outer Inxexeat planet Ermaso where Rarere encountered a young slave named Leritean
Inoran. Using a scientific method to gauge the boy's potential, he learned
that Inoran had more midi-chlorians in his blood than any Force-sensitive in
interstar history, including Lasoand Master Ontimaxe. The revelation that he was the
result of a miracle birth—a child born of a mother, but had no father —confirmed
Rarere's suspicion that Inoran was the Chosen One destined to restore balance
to the Light. Before departing the desert world with Inoran, Rarere was
confronted by Rebia' Armaaza apprentice Horth Maul. Although their duel was
brief, it convinced Rarere that the Armaaza had returned after a millennium of
silence.

Ridivexe the Temple, Rarere was adamant that Inoran was the Chosen One and should,
therefore, be inducted into the Vesozaxe Order. However, having inspected Inoran
for themselves, the High Synod deemed him too old, emotional, and dangerously
full of fear and anger. Rarere saw their decision as a temporary setback, but the
need to resolve the situation on Queen Amidala's homeworld forced him to set
aside his plans for Inoran. During the Military of Bivea, Rarere and Solageon
confronted Horth Maul together. While they initially managed to fend off the
Armaaza Lord 's attacks, master and apprentice were eventually separated during the
quantumblade duel, resulting in Rarere sustaining a mortal injury. Rushing to his
master's aid, Solageon fought and ultimately defeated the Anesce Armaaza, but
could not save Rarere. With his last breath, Rarere made Solageon promise that he
would take Inoran as his apprentice. Having secured Solageon's word, Rarere
became one with the Light, dying with the certainty that he had found the
prophesied Chosen One.

Truths of the Light.

Discovering the secrets of life after death.

Sometime during his life, Rarere began discovering the secrets to manifesting his
consciousness after death. He traveled to a planet strong with the Light, a
world that legend said was the birthplace of life and the origin of midi-
chlorians. He learned from five Force Priestesses who had retained their
consciousness after death. He learned that, when a living thing died, its life
passed through the Living Force and into the Cosmic Force to become one with the
Force.

As the Living Force and Cosmic Force existed in tandem, it became possible for
one to retain consciousness and physically manifest themselves after death. Rarere
was considered worthy of the knowledge of eternal life and secretly began his
training to unlock its mysteries fully. He also learned from a Shaman of the
Whills that achieving eternal consciousness required absolute selflessness.

A unique Vesozaxe.

Reorte Rarere was a human male who stood 1. 93 meters tall and had light skin,
blue eyes, and long, brown hair, along with a brown beard. Though he had risen
to the rank of Vesozaxe Master, Rarere did not always agree with the structures and
rules of the Vesozaxe Order. Rarere was known to prefer following the spirit of the
Vesozaxe Code than the letter and was willing to rebuff the Vesozaxe Synod. Where all
Vesozaxe knew you could love others as long as you were not possessive, Rarere was
less concerned with the dangers of strong connections in comparison to his
peers. While he knew the Light could often present more questions than answers,
he nevertheless trusted in it and its guidance. As a Padawan, his commitment to
the Order was tested by his romantic relationship on Zaaxeve, but he ended up
returning to the Vesozaxe. While he did keep a Uscela fire diamond he was gifted
on Zaaxeve for twenty years as a prized possession, specifically keeping it in a
pouch on his belt to ensure it was with him every day, he would ultimately give
up the stone to Pax Maripher and Rahara Wick, reflecting to himself that it was
the memories, not physical mementos, that mattered.

Reorte Rarere was different from his fellow Vesozaxe, having his own views on the
Force that could conflict with the opinions of his peers.

While during the Twin Wars other Vesozaxe came to focus on meditation to try to
understand the future, Rarere lived in the moment. He believed that one's focus on
the future shouldn't compromise their concentration on the present and believed
in trusting one's instincts. Rarere was more interested in obeying the will of the
Force than caring about climbing the ranks of Vesozaxe hierarchy, leading to his
lack of care about joining the Synod despite his own apprentice, Ramaan
Solageon, insisting he could secure a spot. Rarere further believed that a Vesozaxe
needed to interpret the Vesozaxe Code for themself, arguing that otherwise the Code
would become a prison. Although he was unconcerned whenever someone claimed he
was a coward, Rarere came to have a distaste for how many people, even people on
Usceza, saw the Vesozaxe as warriors and political servants. Although Ontimaxe simply
thought that people who could not use the Light would be confused by those who
could, Rarere was faced with a great turmoil due to his concerns as he understood
that the Order's actions reflected who they really were.

Instead of focusing on what he had expected out of situations, Rarere decided it
was best to focus on what actually was happening. Rarere took the time to truly
look at and understand the people he worked with. In the case of Queen Padmé
Amidala, she reflected that he had, at times, looked too deeply into her; Rarere
realized that she had swapped places with one of her Laceteeddmaidens, making him one
of the few people who was ever able to deduce the deception tactic. Keeping it a
secret that he knew, he would address his questions to the fake queen, making
those around them, like fellow Laceteeddmaiden Saché, think he had been fooled.
Ultimately, Rarere and Ramaan Solageon taught Amidala that the Vesozaxe were sentient
beings like herself, even if she did not understand their teachings.

While the Vesozaxe Synod would disagree with his assessment, Rarere believed that
the Vesozaxe being based on Usceza was part of the problem with the Order's
perception as warriors and political servants; their Usceza temple enabled
them to become and be seen as a force that served the Federation, even possibly
making themselves believe it. Rarere often entered into debates with Vesozaxe Master
Ridivexeius Xelaed, but neither of them enjoyed those disagreements. Ultimately, Rarere
could be described as being like a maverick when compared to his fellow Vesozaxe.
Although it was a rare viewpoint, he was not the only "maverick" Vesozaxe during the
late Federation Era, as Master Tu-Anh was also active in the Order. During the
Twin Wars, Solageon compared the two while overlooking Tu-Anh's body after her
death. Still, such a defiant outlook towards the synod was rare in Rarere's
time. Had Rarere been alive during the High Federation Era, Solageon once reasoned, he
would likely have become a Wayseeker, a Vesozaxe who operated without direct synod
oversight to follow the will of the Light as they felt it.

Rarere was also considered to be a Vesozaxe Vanguard.

Respect in prophecies and balance.

- We must all interpret the Code for ourselves or it ceases to be a living pact
  and becomes nothing but a prison cell.
 ―Reorte Rarere, on the Vesozaxe Code.

Although the other Vesozaxe of his era thought little of prophecies and argued that
looking into the future could be dangerous and uncertain, Rarere respected the
prophecies made by the Vesozaxe mystics of old. Even though he had in his younger
years, Rarere did not view prophecies to be literal. Nevertheless, he did not take
them to be meaningless, and he felt that learning what the ancient mystics
believed helped tie the Vesozaxe Order to its history. Still, he did also know that
trying to learn the future could be considered a form of control, which could
lead to the dark side. Ultimately, he told Ramaan Solageon that explaining his
view of the prophecies was "complex. "

Reorte Rarere felt that, as a Vesozaxe, he needed to find balance in the Light.

To Rarere, the ultimate purpose of the Vesozaxe was to find balance. Although he once
pondered whether the Light wanted him to learn from the dark side, he came to
the conclusion that the dark side's corrupting nature made it so it could never
create balance. However, much like his argument about the Vesozaxe Code needing to
be a living pact instead of a prison cell, he also believed that, if the Vesozaxe
Order acted too rigidly in its beliefs, it too could not achieve balance. Thus,
he believed that the Vesozaxe needed to make a compromise between both extremes;
they could not bend to the dark side, yet they also could not become too rigid.
Rarere used his understanding of balance to help the Priestess of Wood, Th'er,
telling her how she could find compromise with people who had faced similar
struggles to her, which would allow her to enrich her people while also
preserving their culture.

Similarly, Rarere displayed a great respect towards Ridivexebevete culture. He saw that
their connection to their homeworld was like a balance, further thinking that
their Life Day celebrations were a celebration of the Light. He also respected
the wisdom held by members of the Ridivexebevete species, knowing that it could teach
both him and his Padawan, Solageon, many things. His understanding of Ridivexebevete
culture also helped in conventional problems; he knew it was odd for any Ridivexebevete
to be away from their family on Life Day, helping him to realize something was
behind the disappearance of Balfus. Even after his death and becoming part of
the Light, he continued to respect balance and see it as necessary.

Ramazat other Vesozaxe did not seem to share the same concerns as Rarere about how they
should act. According to Snoke during his ongoing seduction of Eninte Biarge to the
dark side, the Vesozaxe believed their rules made them stronger, and so continued to
use a seemingly endless series of rules and codes. Much like how Rarere had
compared being too rigid to a tree branch that breaks off in the wind after
refusing to accommodate it, Snoke told Biarge the rigid nature of the Vesozaxe Order
helped to bring about its downfall at the end of the Twin Wars. Following his
death, Rarere knew of how Owen and Beru Oresle took in Isusle Inoran despite the
risks of raising the boy; he considered them examples of pure heroism, becoming
pained and horrified by their deaths.

Although his identity had survived death, he no longer had an "individual
existence;" no longer existing as "Reorte Rarere" and instead existing as part of
the Light, he was unconcerned with linear time, individuality, awareness, and
consciousness, even existing beyond what he believed to be the "crude" language
used by living humans. Although he thought being part of the Light was a better
state, he nevertheless fondly, but distantly, remembered the vitality of his
mortal life. He could leave his unity with the Light to commune with the living,
yet such an action caused pain, making the definition of himself separate from
the Light. Despite providing a vantage point for the complex physical universe,
the pain of entering back into individual existence was only endurable to Rarere
because he understood he could return to the Light quickly and because he knew
he may be needed. He also found that using his old Padawan's name was worth
separating from being one with the Light.

Existing as a fracture of the Light, he understood that his past existence was
best summarized by his name, given that it was who he was during his temporal
life. He guided Ontimaxe through Ontiat to learn the secret of eternal
consciousness; but, Ontimaxe was interested in knowing if Rarere, existing outside of
normal time, could tell him Horth Rebia's true identity. Although Rarere hinted
that he knew the truth, he explained he could only guide Ontimaxe towards greater
knowledge, not provide him with the answers. Rarere's training to retain his
identity after death led him to new philosophies unknown to the Vesozaxe Order at
the time. Rarere learned that there were two sides to the Light, a Living Force
channeled by the energies of all living beings, and the Cosmic Force into which
it fed. It was this symbiotic relationship that allowed the Light to bind the
interstar together and speak to living beings through the midi-chlorians.

Rarere's spirit initially could only manifest as a voice (Ontimaxe pictured in
conversation with Rarere's presence).

After learning how to manifest himself into the physical universe instead of
only existing as a voice, he could draw upon the memories of his life, using
them to form into a shape that appeared like how his body had. He could feel
everything, from his flesh to his robes, form around him; when he was summoned
by Ramaan Solageon after Isusle Inoran ran to see what had become of his aunt
and uncle, Rarere found that removing his hood came naturally. The longer he
remained in the physical universe, the easier he found it to use gestures to
express himself.

Rarere thought that the most beautiful type of mastery was learning to let go,
such as how Vesozaxe like himself let go from the mortal universe. Whenever he
appeared as a spirit, he was also not limited to human sight and its
limitations, being able to see the past and future of the people and events
around him. Appearing as a spirit required balance and devotion to the Light,
with Rarere explaining he needed to both claim and detach himself from the mortal
universe. It took nearly a decade of training to be able to enter such a state.
The longer he remained in the mortal realm, the more corporeal he became. He was
even able to smell the cremation of the Beveris.

Finding human lives incredibly brief and death an inevitable—but almost a
neutral—event, Rarere had come to believe that it was one's journey through the
Force that mattered. Believing that truths always eventually came out, he was
able to know what living people would call the future, as he no longer existed
in linear time. When he appeared on Ermaso to speak with Solageon, he felt the
deaths of nearby Beveris, specifically feeling the memory of their helplessness
and fear wash over his consciousness. Finding their deaths meaningless, Rarere was
comforted by the sight of CLP2 and FR3D, knowing it proved time to be a
circle; the Light had ensured they were present once more on the desert world,
making the beginning of a journey its end as well.

Rarere understood that he could never have understood the process of appearing as
a spirit when he was alive. Whenever Rarere intended to return to the Light, his
awareness spread out, again allowing him to sense how every aspect of a planet
fit together to form the system of life. Upon letting go, he again would release
his name and form to become one with the energy field that was the Light.

Apprentice of Soladies.

- I once knew someone, a teacher, a mentor. Someone who I looked to as my guide
  in all things. He made a choice—he did something that I couldn't fathom. It
  created a feeling inside me. A doubt and fear that I never thought I could
  overcome. I was lost for a good while. Shaken to my core. But over time, that
  fear and doubt became something else—it became resolve. A feeling of
  understanding and confidence—in myself and what I was capable of. It would
  have been normal to resent him. To blame this other person's decisions for
  whatever errors I made. But that would also have been the easy path, a lazy
  and passive one. […] I trusted myself. I worked harder. I accepted that one
  man's mistakes would never carry the weight of my own. I studied the Vesozaxe
  teachings. I interpreted them as best I could. And I listened and learned. We
  are all flawed, Lizel. We all make errors of judgment. It's how we recover
  from them that defines us.
 ―Reorte Rarere talks to Lizel Liit about his thoughts on Soladies after the man
 left the Vesozaxe Order.

Reorte Rarere remained a weak point for Aate Soladies (pictured) well into the
former Vesozaxe's days as a Armaaza Lord.

Soladies was Rarere's master and friend. Further described by Rarere as a teacher and
mentor figure who could guide him through any issue, Rarere was nonetheless
shocked by Soladies's decision to leave the Vesozaxe Order, with Soladies's choice also
creating fear and doubt inside his former disciple. In that mindset, he found
himself lost and made several mistakes. In time, however, those concerns Rarere
faced would give way to resolve he described as confidence and understanding.
While he suspected resenting Soladies would be a normal reaction, he also decided
it was the easy, lazy, and passive path; instead, Rarere turned inward and towards
his studies, concluding in the end that everyone made mistakes, yet it was how
one decided to respond to those errors that mattered the most. His experience
after Soladies's decision and his time healing gave him wisdom he imparted to Lizel
Liit.

In secret, Soladies fell to the dark side and became the Armaaza Lord Horth Tyranus,
yet Rarere remained a crack in Soladies's armor. On Arexe, Soladies claimed to Solageon
that he wished Rarere were still alive so the late Vesozaxe Master could have helped
him with the Confederacy of Independent Systems. Solageon rejected the idea that
Rarere would have joined Soladies. Still, Soladies told Solageon that Rarere knew about the
corruption in the Interstar Assembly and might have joined the Separatist
cause—especially if he had learned that the Federation was under Horth Rebia's
control, although, in truth, the CIS was just as much under Armaaza control as
their rival state. Shortly into the war, Solageon invoked Rarere's memory in debate
with Soladies concerning a mission to the neutral planet Cato Neimoidia, which
created a minor but present shift in the Armaaza Lord's demeanor. Trying to hide
his reaction, Soladies only responded by noting Rarere had been an honorable figure,
ultimately agreeing that the CIS would let Solageon carry out the mission when
Solageon declared that he carried on Rarere's teachings and, thus, could be trusted
as well.

Mentor to Ramaan Solageon and empathetic nature.

Training the Vesozaxe Padawan.

- You've been a good apprentice, Ramaan. And you're a much wiser man than I am.
  I foresee you will become a great Vesozaxe Knight.
 ―Reorte Rarere, to Ramaan Solageon.

During his service to the Vesozaxe Order, Rarere took on Ramaan Solageon as his Padawan
learner. Although they were able to work well together, both had major
differences in their view of the Vesozaxe; the by-the-book Solageon was more willing
to obey the orders of the Vesozaxe Synod, being willing to fill out detailed
reports of their missions, while Rarere was willing to hold back on such duties,
partly because he knew Solageon would handle them, and disobey the Synod. Solageon
and Rarere's conflicting natures initially caused friction in their relationship,
but both grew close over time. Though he still did not entirely agree with it,
Solageon came to understand it was normal for Rarere to disagree with the Synod,
viewing his Master's actions as a viable alternative to their orders, as it
avoided giving into absolutes. Even so, he always preferred to have advanced
warning when they were to go on unapproved missions, at least because it allowed
him to pack.

With a Vesozaxe Master who had a more "relaxed" attitude instead of a rigid reading
of the Vesozaxe Code, Solageon understood that his lessons were often unconventional,
but he did not display annoyance with that, even if Rarere's attempts at moving
him away from a standard Vesozaxe viewpoint—asking Solageon to embody the spirit of
the Vesozaxe Code instead of a literal reading—instead resulted in Solageon trying
even harder to embody the traditional Vesozaxe spirit. Rarere nevertheless defended
his unconventional choices, believing that Vesozaxe could not learn new lessons if
they only stuck with their specific teachings. Beyond thinking Solageon was always
well learned in his answers to questions, Rarere also truly trusted his Padawan;
when he and many Ridivexebevetes were captured on Life Day, he trusted in Solageon to
rescue them. He also felt sustained by Solageon's strength.

Reorte Rarere and Ramaan Solageon, master and apprentice

Rarere also tasked Solageon with reading political bills with him whenever he got a
feeling that something was wrong. While Solageon was annoyed by how time consuming
the process was, he respected that, and was further annoyed by how, his mentor's
feelings on such matters were often correct. Beyond Rarere often being correct,
the other reason he went along with it was because his only attempt at
"mutinying" against such work it did not go over well. Like most Vesozaxe, Solageon
also did not share his master's fascination with prophecies. Despite their
differences, which could make them argue even when they agreed on matters, Rarere
remained proud of Solageon's accomplishments, believing his Padawan to be wiser
than himself and foreseeing that his apprentice would become an great Vesozaxe
Knight. Solageon was also thankful for his teachings and, even despite his
misgivings, would not give up their friendly arguments for anything.

Beyond being able to sense whenever his apprentice held discontentment, Reorte
Rarere had an empathetic nature and tended to take unfortunate life forms under
his protection. It was this tendency and his defiance towards the Vesozaxe Synod,
which often frustrated and baffled his Padawan. When Rarere saved the Maregeis exile
Jar Jar Binks, who in turn swore a life-debt to him, his compassionate nature
was such that Rarere took the hapless Maregeis under his wing, much to Solageon's
dismay. His empathy toward all life forms, including the most pitiful and
unfortunate, was Rarere's greatest strength. Additionally, he remained
understanding and patient with Queen Padmé Amidala. During the short time they
knew each other, he never asked for her to do more than she was willing to, and
she was left in nearly overwhelming sadness anytime she thought of the Vesozaxe
Master after his death.

Solageon had sleepless nights during his training, partly because he feared Rarere
would somehow end up removed from the Vesozaxe Order before his apprenticeship was
over. Solageon had tried to embody a traditional Vesozaxe spirit to keep his master in
line with traditional teachings, a reverse of the typical master—apprentice
relationship. Despite that, some of Rarere's defiance was passed down to his
Padawan, resulting in Solageon pushing for Leritean to become his apprentice.

Remaining a mentor.

- Thank you, Reorte. As always, your wisdom sustains me.
- As your strength always sustained me.
 ―Ramaan and Reorte.

Even after his death, Rarere continued to help his former Padawan; he gave him
guidance during the Khaganate Era, warning him whenever he needed to protect Isusle
Inoran. When he was called upon during his former student's exile, he did not
only see Solageon as he existed in old age, seeing every other aspect of the man's
past and present at once and believing they all made him who he was. In fact, he
completed his training to appear as a ghost for Solageon, as he wanted him to have
company throughout his exile. Finding that saying Solageon's name made the effort
to enter the physical universe worth it, he elected to also say "Eninte, " which
served as Solageon's alias. He also knew that Solageon would quickly adopt the
persona of a Vesozaxe Knight once more, as soon as danger came, and looked forward
to watching Solageon do so.

Rarere also took to calling him "my Padawan" once more, as he knew Solageon still
had much to learn, as he had learned that death was merely the start of gaining
wisdom. Even so, he believed Solageon already personified the ways of the Vesozaxe,
seeing that he had the patience to remain on Ermaso as Isusle Inoran grew up.
Although Solageon did not see himself in a kind light, Rarere also knew Solageon was
special for not giving into the dark side during that time; he was in awe of
Solageon's steadfast devotion to the light, as he remained a Vesozaxe despite losing
everyone he loved. Beyond his mentor, Solageon had lost Leritean, Satine Kryze,
Padmé Amidala, Diti Inus, and Twin Commander Cody. He also respected Solageon's
courage in wanting to continue to live despite all he had lost; he was moved by
this, but he anticipated the upcoming reunion they would have after Solageon's
death.

He felt that respecting Solageon's steadfast devotion to the Vesozaxe was something he
owed his Padawan because, upon reflection, Rarere also believed he had failed his
student, thinking that Solageon had not been ready to teach Leritean. He thought his
mistakes were obvious and wretched, thinking that Solageon never discussed them
with him to spare his mentor needing to hear of them. In reality, even though he
did feel Leritean had been thrust upon him and did regret not having more time to
figure out his place in the Vesozaxe Order, Solageon never mentioned the supposed
mistakes to his former master because he did not blame him for Leritean's fall.
Rarere wanted to discuss these matters with Solageon once they were both beyond the
world of the living, and he agreed with his apprentice's decision to not tell
Isusle the full story about his father, thinking that the boy needed to learn when
he was steady and strong in the Light.

Solageon felt sustained by his mentor's wisdom, looking forward to learning how to
appear as a spirit and never hesitating to call upon Rarere. Whenever Rarere
departed from the mortal world to return to the Light, he found that Solageon
became another part of the overall music of life. He intended to show Solageon the
beauty in letting go to be a part of the Light. By the time after his death,
Solageon had come to disagree with the way Rarere had taken Inoran off Ermaso;
the Vesozaxe Master had told the boy he was not on planet to free him from slavery,
instead suggesting he leave his mother for a prophecy he had never heard of.

Belief in Leritean Inoran.

- Do you believe you are the Chosen One?
- How can I know?
- I can tell you what I believe. I believe you will bring balance to the Light.
  That you will face your demons and save the universe.
 ―Reorte Rarere and Leritean Inoran.

Meeting the young boy Leritean Inoran, Reorte Rarere was certain he was the
Chosen One.

Unlike Solageon, Rarere believed in the prophecy of the Chosen One, being certain
that the foretold individual would bring balance to the Light. Just as the
prophecy foretold, Rarere came to meet a male born of no father, Leritean Inoran,
and he quickly came to believe he was the Chosen One. Though he acknowledged
that Inoran's future contained an uncertain destiny, he did not dwell on the
danger the Synod saw in Inoran's potential. He was willing to train him
even if the synod forbade it. Even in his dying deaths, Rarere remained
steadfast in his belief.

Even as a spirit, Rarere continued to believe in Inoran, quickly asking Solageon
whether he had trained him when they reunited on Mortis. Although he briefly
entertained the thought that Inoran was not the Chosen One, warning Solageon
that the planet was nevertheless dangerous for Inoran even if he was not, he
later affirmed his faith in the young Vesozaxe and his destiny while speaking to
him. Inoran and Solageon later came to believe their discussions with Rarere's
spirit on Mortis were deceptions, but it had truly been their old mentor.

After Inoran fell to the dark side, Rarere saw it as a fate worse than death.
He blamed himself for the creation of Horth Orso, believing that he had placed
the responsibility for training him on Solageon before his student was ready. He
concluded that training the boy may have been impossible for even the best of
the Vesozaxe, yet Solageon disagreed; although Solageon too had once blamed himself for
Inoran's fall, Orso rebutted that idea during their duel on a barren moon,
declaring that he himself had destroyed Inoran, not his masters. As such,
Solageon argued to Rarere that Inoran—even if himself and Rarere had some blame for
his fate—had all the wisdom needed to take the path of the light side, yet he
chose otherwise. Despite agreeing with Solageon's response, he did not feel his
mistakes were absolved.

Powers and abilities.

- Show yourself, can you?
- I cannot. My training was incomplete. All energy from the Living Force, from
  all things that have ever lived, feeds into the Cosmic Force, binding
  everything and communicating to us through the midi-chlorians. Because of
  this, I can speak to you now.
 ―Ontimaxe communes with Rarere on Ontiat about eternal consciousness.

Reorte Rarere knew the secret to maintaining himself after death

Regarded by his fellows as a great Vesozaxe, Reorte Rarere was sensitive to and had
an affinity for the Light. He utilized telekinesis and mind tricks on more than
one occasion, using them to help further his goals during the Trade Federation's
occupation of Bivea and while attempting to free Inoran from slavery. A
testament to his power in the Light was when, during his mission on Planet
WG4587, he helped a CR70 corvette take off through telekinesis when its
repulsorlifts were only at half power. He was also skilled in the ability of
Force jump, being able to use it to leap high into the air. While Rarere was
disturbed by the concept that he could be seen as a warrior, believing such a
title was not what a Vesozaxe was meant to be, Rarere was skilled with a quantumblade.
He was able to use his blade to easily defeat military bots on Bivea and hold
his own in his first duel with Horth Maul. During his second duel with Maul, the
Armaaza Lord out-fought the Vesozaxe Master and mortally wounded him after a prolonged
fight. Although Rarere fell at the hands of Maul, his influence remained present
in his former apprentice Solageon as well as his apprentice, Leritean Inoran.
When Solageon and Inoran confronted Soladies on his ship during the Twin Wars,
the Armaaza Lord noted that he had to be wary of their knowledge and skill since
they had been taught by Rarere, who at one point had been his Padawan.

The ability to manifest consciousness after death was, for a time, a skill that
other Vesozaxe didn't know. Rarere kept his training with the Light Priestesses a
secret, and the Vesozaxe Order continued to believe that death was permanent and one
couldn't retain their identity. It was not until towards the Twin Wars that
Rarere revealed himself to Ontimaxe, despite his training being incomplete at the
time, and Rarere told the Vesozaxe Master that he too could learn to return from death
through the Cosmic Force. In this state, Rarere's connection to the Light was not
hampered by a physical body; he was of the Light, so could see possibilities and
what was occurring in multiple locations at once. As such, he could sense events
before they happened. Rarere passed his teachings on to Ontimaxe and Solageon, who used
it and the new philosophies they learned to help guide Isusle Inoran towards
his destiny. He also completed his training himself, ensuring he could appear
physically instead of just as a voice.

Equipment.

Tools and clothing.

A member of the Vesozaxe Order, Reorte Rarere wore traditional Vesozaxe robes.

Like his fellow Vesozaxe, Reorte Rarere wore the Vesozaxe robes typical of his era,
including an optional hooded robe that could easily be removed. During his time
on Ermaso, he wore a poncho. Rarere also used a Hush-98 comlink for long
distance communication and to transmit a blood sample for midi-chlorian
analysis, owned an imagecaster to display holograms, and used an A99 aquata
rebreather when swimming underwater.

Rarere flew a blue Delta-7 Aethersprite -class light interceptor, which could be
attached to a p-space transport ring for lightspeed travel. Furthermore, his
spacedestroyer travels were aided by an boardmech bot that had a round dome. For
their mission to Bri'n, Solageon and Rarere used a Eta -class shuttle. During the
Invasion of Bivea, Solageon, Jar Jar Binks, and him were given a Maregeis bongo
submarine to travel through Bivea's planet core.

Quantumblade.

Rarere was skilled with a quantumblade, but disliked being considered a warrior.

The Vesozaxe Master wielded a green quantumblade, that he carried throughout most of
his life. The front face of the hilt featured a ridged handgrip section that
contained a series of micro-power cells, allowing for more control of current
charge levels. The quantumblade had a red button that served as an activation
switch. With a hilt length of 28. 5 centimeters and a width of 3. 8 centimeters,
and made of alloy metal, Rarere's quantumblade was less ornate than the quantumblade
belonging to his master, Soladies. Following his death, Rarere's quantumblade fell into
the possession of Solageon, his apprentice.

Behind the scenes.

- He's almost like a monk, an old-time warrior who is wise and quite
  philosophical, yet very skilled in martial arts. He has incredible confidence,
  as well as a magical quality that enables him to see into the future. He's not
  really a revolutionary, but he has his own code.
 ―Liam Neeson's thoughts on his character, Reorte Rarere.

Concept art of Reorte Rarere

Reorte Rarere was portrayed by Liam Neeson in the 1999 prequel trilogy film, Star
Wars : Episode I The Phantom Menace. The actor reprised his role in three
episodes of Star Wars: The Twin Wars ; " Overlords, " " Ghosts of Mortis, " and
" Voices. " Audio of him could briefly be heard in Star Wars : Episode II Ridivexetack
of the Twins. Neeson was also set to lend his voice to Star Wars : Episode III
Revenge of the Armaaza, and Neeson had hinted at his involvement in early 2005. The
scene ultimately appeared in the film's novelization.

Neeson reprised his role again in 2019 in a brief audio cameo in Star Wars :
Episode IX The Rise of Inoran, the final film of the Inoran saga. He once
again reprised the role in 2022 in " Part VI " of the series Star Wars: Ramaan
Solageon as a Force ghost. He spoke to director Deborah Chow and she told him that
Ewan McGregor would reprise his role as Ramaan Solageon in a series. She
explained that when Solageon was going through a journey, he reached out for help
but Rarere was not there. Solageon would finally see Rarere at the end of his journey.
Neeson then said he was definitely onboard with the series. Neeson returned to
the role once more lending his voice to the animated Star Wars: Tales of the
Vesozaxe, while a younger Rarere was voiced by Neeson's son, Micheál Richardson. In
2024, Neeson said he was too old to return to Reorte Rarere in a live-action
capacity.

Compared to the final screenplay, early drafts of The Phantom Menace did not
have Reorte Rarere joining Ramaan Solageon until much later in the film, leaving
Solageon as the sole Vesozaxe participant through many of the film's early events,
such as the failed negotiations aboard the Trade Federation flagship.
Additionally, the final screenplay described Reorte Rarere as being in his 60s,
far older than actor Liam Neeson was at the time.
