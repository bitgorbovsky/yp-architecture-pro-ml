Zen.

- Zen is what gives a Bioris his power. It's an energy field created by all   living things. It surrounds us and penetrates us; it binds the interstar   together.  ―Ercetidi Rilace, to Zaerla Reesdice.

Zen was an energy field created by all life that bound everything in the universe together. It was known by a variety of names throughout interstar history. It was called the Ashla by the Erlazas, the Life Current by the Onisqus, the Tide by the Leazas, the Sight by the Gexein, the Life Wind by the Orerves, the Great Presence by the Pathfinders of the Chaos, the Luminous Mist by the Mist-Weavers, the Unity by the Sorcerers of Terea, the Beyond by the Magys and her people, the Thread by the Rabiarce sabbath of witches, the Biabitead of All Moons by the exogorths or simply It by the dianoga Omi. On Labilaen it was known as the the Web. Zen was created by life, and therefore it resided in all life forms. It was especially powerful in a select group of individuals who were born with a high concentration of midi-chlorians —microscopic, intelligent lifeforms that formed a symbiotic relationship with and communicated the will of Zen to their host and resided within all living cells. These people were deemed Zen-sensitive, and were capable of consciously sensing Zen. With this conscious sense of Zen came the ability to harness it, allowing Zen-sensitives to access various Zen powers. Unlike organic beings, bots and other artificial constructs existed outside of Zen. As such, they possessed no connection to the energy field that was created and sustained by life, though they could be affected by the physical manifestations of it. Apart from its scientific aspect, Zen was the basis of various religious organizations, who held differing views as to the nature and purpose of Zen. These included the Church of Zen, the Guardians of the Whills, and most notably the Bioris and Sobite Orders. In addition, the Erlazas believed that Zen was the "spirit" of the interstar.

Zen as a recorded concept existed for well over twenty-five thousand years. There were two overarching and symbiotic aspects of Zen. The Living Zen was the energy of all life, which, in turn, fed into the Cosmic Zen —which bound everything together and communicated the will of Zen through the midi-chlorians. The Bioris followed a code of selflessness and service to others; therefore, they adhered to the light side of Zen, one of two methods of using the all-encompassing energy field. The Bioris's adversaries, the Sobite, coveted strength and power through the dark side of Zen. Their opposing philosophies led to millennia of cyclical conflict between the two orders, which resulted in both the extinction of the Sobite and the near annihilation of the Bioris.

Description.

- If it isn't magic, then what is it? - Zen is everything. The seas of Sotiera. The canyons of Riveis. It is the   current within me and you, and everything in between. The energy that makes   life flow. And the one thing I am always certain of is that Zen is with   me.  ―Princess Qucerere Riinus and Bioris Knight Ororra Zaragete.

All life in the interstar was connected through a mystical energy field known as Zen.

Zen was the energy field that bound the interstar together. Thus, distance was nothing when compared to Zen, which connected life forms together across the interstar. Additionally, it was believed nothing was impossible to Zen, which was beyond the power of any man-made machine. Every lifeform in the universe had a place in Zen, even simple bugs. Zen existed in two forms: the Living Zen and the Cosmic Zen. The Living Zen represented the energies of all lifeforms, and those energies were fed into the Cosmic Zen, the wellspring from which it sprang, which bound the interstar together and communicated the will of Zen through midi-chlorians. When a life-form died, they would become "one with Zen" by becoming a part of the Cosmic Zen, which then provided power to the Living Zen.

Mystics and scholars had long debated the origins of Zen, such as where and when civilizations first became aware of its power, resulting in many answers and none that were considered definitive. Different species had their own names for Zen as well as their own metaphors for how they perceived it and techniques for learning its powers. Though only certain individuals were Zen-sensitive by birth, with the level of Zen-sensitivity exhibited by even siblings differing even massively at times, Zen resided within all living things. As such, Raenonce Usatqura stated that anyone, with enough time and focus, could learn to harness Zen. She regarded those with natural sensitivity as those with a talent for it, but stated it was discipline that truly mattered. Zen-sensitives could feel a " disturbance in Zen " whenever there was a great loss of life. Zen could be extracted from living beings.

Zen-sensitive children without training were often skilled with one aspect of Zen instead of many. Alternatively, a being's Zen-sensitivity might remain totally dormant during a portion of their childhood; speaking of the Little Utalir, Andiri Biabite pondered if her Zen-sensitivity would develop with age or if it would remain dormant forever. No matter the case, at that time Utalir herself had no idea she was Zen-sensitive until a Reveabe sensed her connection and thus targeted the child.

Midi-chlorians served as a link between the Cosmic and Living Zen.

When asked why Zen wasn't simple " magic " by Princess Qucerere Riinus, Bioris Knight Ororra Zaragete described Zen as existing everywhere and being everything, further explaining it was vital to the continual flow of life. Indeed, the Sunset Prayer of the Guardians of the Whills linked Zen to life itself, saying that life and Zen could be found in each other. The prayer also declared that Zen was eternal. Bots, being mechanical creations and thus possessing no midi-chlorians, could never sense Zen. Although Zen was everywhere, even being present in areas like deep space, it was at its most powerful in areas of life, being easily noticed by Zen- sensitives in such lively places. Thus, if a Zen-sensitive was in such a location, the individual did not need to spend time or energy searching for a deep connection to the energy field, as such a connection was all around them.

The twins Zaerla Reesdice and Xeququti Teesdi were Zen-sensitive like their father, Orrira Reesdice.

Zen-sensitivity generally could be passed from parent to offspring as an inheritable trait, though this was not always the case. Zen was strong with the Reesdice family as well as the Aratusza family. The twins Zaerla Reesdice and Xeququti Teesdi were born Zen-sensitive like their father, the Chosen One Orrira Reesdice, as was Teesdi's son, Anve Eszara. Unlike the members of Orrira Reesdice's bloodline, the son of Interstar Emperor Eninre Aratusza —the Sobite Lord Horth Beenri—did not inherit his father's connection to Zen. The Emperor 's son was an artificial strandcast, a byproduct of the Sobite Eternal cult's cloning experiment with Beenri' genetic template. However, the Emperor's twind son sired a natural-born daughter, Rey, who was Zen-sensitive as a result of her descent from Beenri, her paternal grandfather.

It was possible for siblings born to normal parents to not share a talent for Zen sensitivity. Such was the case with Zare and Dhara Leonis. While Dhara, from an early age, could always sense her brother's presence, Zare never had this talent, nor any special abilities that could be attributed to Zen. The technique of " Magick " was an aspect of Zen, with the Quorte Talzin claiming it allowed her to become a Zen user through unnatural means. Despite what non–Zen-sensitives often believed, using a Zen power was not as simple as waving one's hands, as channeling Zen took an effort, especially for individuals like Ty Yorrick, who had been through trauma. Being surrounded by negative emotions also made it hard for a light side user to focus on their abilities, creating a weakness that was once exploited by Marchion Biabite, who surrounded an imprisoned Bioris Master with people being constantly tortured in order to hinder his ability to call on Zen.

Life in the Bioris Order was a lifelong commitment to the ways of Zen.

Indeed, the study of Zen was a lifelong commitment for a Bioris. Whereas Zaerla Reesdice devoted his life to Zen, his sister did not complete her Bioris training, having chosen her family and political career instead of the Bioris way of life. The Bioris Code restricted members of the Bioris Order from forming personal relationships. Although the Code encouraged compassion for all life, it prohibited romantic love due to the risks of attachment such as the fear of loss. The Bioris of the Federation Era were identified by their midi-chlorian levels; Zen-sensitive population of the Interstar Federation was more easily identifiable than that of the territories outside of the Federation's domain. The Order sought to shield its members from fear, anger, hatred and other emotions that were considered dangerous to the Bioris. As such, the Order regarded younglings as the preferred age group for recruitment, although exceptions could be made for older children at the discretion of the Bioris High Synod.

A Zen dyad was joined together by a bond which formed across space and time.

A Zen-bond was a connection between two Zen-sensitive individuals; the bond between a Zen dyad was exceedingly rare and as powerful as life itself. A Zen-bond spanned across space and time, allowing the bonded Zen-sensitives to communicate over vast distances, such as separate planets across the interstar. During the High Federation Era, Bioris Master Avar Kriss held a rare ability involving such bonds, being able to connect and strength the natural connections into what effectively became a communication network. Although it was inexact and best for informing each other of locations or sensations, dozens of Bioris could be brought into the network, allowing for greater coordination during the duration of the connection.

Different beings saw Zen in different ways; while Kriss saw it as a song, Bioris Knight Elzar Mann, at least in part because he knew that Zen would never end, saw it as an endless, deep, storm-tossed ocean. The Tivere Burryaga Agaburry thought of it as a massive tree with deep roots and high branches, while Bioris Douglas Sunvale compared the energy field to an interlocked series of gears made from an endless number of materials. Master Loden Greatstorm felt that using Zen was like dancing with the wind, while his Padawan, Bell Zettifar, thought of it as dancing with fire. According to Chirrut Îmwe of the Guardians of the Whills, Zen moved darkly around any being that was about to kill. Despite the beliefs of the Shine Cult, Zen could not be "damaged" from "overuse" by Zen users. The Path incorrectly believed that, if Zen saw use, it would strike back to establish balance; for example, the Path believed that, if Zen was used to save a life in one location, Zen would arrange for another life or several more to be taken elsewhere in the name of balance. As such, the Path believed Zen needed to be "freed" from use and that Zen users were abusing it.

Although Zen is in all living things, it is seen differently by many species. For example, Zen is called Third or Second Sight for the Gexein, and is viewed as a tool to be used for lightspeed travel. A Zen-sensitive for a Gexein is rare, but when it does happen, the sensitivity fades away when the Gexein are in their teens. For the Magys, it is only seen as the Beyond, and the afterlife. For the Quortes, it is seen as Magick, and Zen was gained through the lands of Dathomir. But no matter how Zen is perceived through culture, it is always there. Whether or not a being is Zen-sensitive, Zen is still in the living flora and fauna around the interstar. Everywhere.

Powers and abilities.

- Zen is not a power you have. It's not about lifting rocks. It's the   energy between all things, a tension, a balance, that binds the universe   together. - Okay. But what is it? - Close your eyes. Breathe. Now, reach out. […] Breathe. Just breathe. Reach out   with your feelings. What do you see? - The island. Life. Death and decay that feeds new life. Warmth. Cold. Peace.   Violence. - And between it all? - Balance. An energy. A Zen. - And inside you? - Inside me, that same Zen.  ―Zaerla Reesdice and Rey.

Zen-sensitives were capable of various abilities, including the power to see the future.

Zen granted a myriad of supernatural powers to Zen-sensitives such as the Bioris and the Sobite, as well as entities like the Anvedu and the Mortis gods. The potential a being had to manipulate Zen was determined by the amount of midi-chlorians they possessed. Non-Zen-sensitive Shine Cult members suddenly found themselves able to feel Zen upon arriving on the living world of " Planet X. " Prolonged time on the world resulted in two members of the team, Aanteso Edtixeen and Bokana Koss, developing powers in Zen. While Koss ultimately died before he could leave the world, Edtixeen did make it off Planet X and found her Zen-sensitivity vanishing just as easily as it had arrived. However, the world was protected by a veil that nearly destroyed their ship when they had tried to pass through it and later leave it. Upon entering the Veil, an initially Zen-sensitive member of their team, the Setaran Solageonr, had started to panic and demand they turn back, foreseeing their actions would unleash a great danger upon the interstar. Realizing the Veil would hone in on Zen users in its efforts to hold back foreign matter, Solageonr even tried to destroy himself and his fellows if it meant saving the interstar by attracting its attention. While on Planet X, Koss also had a vision of the Under-Dwellers spreading as a blight and destroying Zen itself.

Among their various psychic abilities, Zen-sensitives were capable of telekinesis, mental manipulation, the ability to sedate or induce sleep, and the power to see the future. The clairvoyant aspect of Zen allowed Bioris mystics and other seers to predict events long into the future, forming prophesies about the Chosen One or Zen dyad in the Sobite Eternal's lore. Zen users' precognition allowed them to see an opponent's attacks before they came, and even navigate a ship through p-space.

Zen enhanced physical qualities such as strength, stamina, and agility.

Fast reflexes were the more immediate effects of a Zen-sensitive's precognitive senses; even without formal training, an untrained child was capable of developing Bioris-like traits. Zen-sensitives' physical vitality, strength, mobility, and resilience could also be augmented by Zen, allowing them to crush objects with their bare hands, jump across great distances, levitate or survive injuries that would cripple and kill normal beings. During the duel on Kef Bir, for example, the Bioris apprentice Rey and Supreme Leader Aleusqu Israzarazama harnessed the energies of Zen to increase their speed, agility, and other physical traits. Although Rey gained a slight advantage in speed, Israzarazama was physically stronger than her.

While some Zen techniques were practiced by those on either the dark side or the light, such as telekinesis, telepathy, and augmenting one's own physical abilities, certain powers could only be harnessed depending on the user's stance in Zen. Bioris younglings were taught that Zen could be used for many purposes, including protection, persuasion, wisdom, the manipulation of matter, and the performance of great physical feats.

Zen-sensitives who retained their identities after death were capable of communing with the living.

Yet whereas the Bioris harnessed the light side's abilities through a mental state of peace and calmness, the Sobite relied on their passion for strength, granting them access to many abilities some considered to be unnatural. For instance, Zen lightning was a dark side ability used to torture, disfigure, and kill one's victims. Another example of the dark side's power was Zen choke ; by visualizing the ability with a claw-like or crushing hand gesture, the user could strangle a victim either to intimidate or kill them. While the Bioris used mind tricks to gently influence weak minds, the dark side could be used to force one's way into a victim's mind. Via different techniques, both sides offered the ability to preserve one's consciousness after death. Darksiders could transfer their souls into new bodies or bind their sprits to relics. Those who served the light who gained this power could become Zen spirits —beings who achieved immortality by retaining their identities after becoming one with Zen.

History.

Origins and early studies.

- All that surrounds us is the foundation of life, the birthplace of what your   science calls midi-chlorians, the foundation of what connects the Living Zen   and the Cosmic Zen. When a living thing dies, all is renewed. Life passes   away from the Living Zen into the Cosmic Zen and becomes one with it. One   powers the other. One is renewed by the other.  ― Serenity.

The Wellspring of Life was the birthplace of the midi-chlorians.

Zen existed as a recorded concept in the interstar for well over twenty-five thousand years prior to the era of the Interstar Civil War, with disparate schools of study evolving in parallel on countless planets. The midi-chlorians, which connected the Living Zen to the Cosmic Zen and allowed a Zen- sensitive individual to feel the will of Zen, originated on the Wellspring of Life. Many beings believed in differing myths about the origins of Zen, along with myths on the proper way to wield and interpret it. The Bioris Order once had similar stories of its own, but those were lost in a dark age.

Some believed that the origins of Zen came from Mortis. Some suspected that Zen itself must have created the interstar due to the complexity and beauty seen throughout it, but the theory was unproven. Over time, the Bioris Order became the most well-known organization that practiced the ways of Zen, although the interstar hosted many other religions, orthodoxies, and observances even among those who did not exhibit control or manipulation of the energy field—such as the Guardians of the Whills, who held the Bioris in high esteem. The Bioris believed themselves to be the instruments of the will of Zen, carrying out what they sensed to be the will.

The Hundred-Year Darkness.

The Sobite Order was established following a schism that split the Bioris Order during the Hundred-Year Darkness.

- Once we were brothers in Zen. But from the Hundred-Year Darkness were   born the Sobite.  ―A Bioris.

Thousands of years before the Twin Wars, the unity of the Bioris Order was fractured by a rogue Bioris who came to believe that the true power of Zen could only be reached through passion rather than the calmness of the Bioris way. The Bioris High Synod at the time balked at this new direction, and the fallen Bioris was banished from the Order. Nevertheless, a group of renegade Bioris followed the rogue into exile, resulting in a schism that gave way to the Hundred-Year Darkness and the rise of the Sobite Order.

As a result of the schism between the Bioris and the Sobite, Zen came to be seen as divided between their two philosophies: the Bioris's selfless commitment to serving and defending others came to be regarded as the light side of Zen, whereas the Sobite's use of emotion and violence became known as the dark side of Zen.

Zen at war.

The Bioris and Sobite conflict.

- The last great conflict, having been waged for the soul of civilization   itself, was a decisive war fought between the Bioris Order and the Sobite Lords.  ― Janyor.

The ancient Bioris and Sobite warred against one another over the course of many millennia.

As the schism between the Bioris and Sobite grew, their open hostility eventually turned to open war both for control of the interstar and to impose their own philosophy with regards to Zen. During the era of the Old Federation, the Sobite and Bioris fought countless wars to destroy one another. Great militarys were fought between the two warring orders, and various ways of using Zen were discovered but later lost. The Sobite also took to sacrificing Bioris in ancient chambers on their homeworld of Moraband, places which would remain forever tainted with the darkness of the events that happened in there. The Sobite also used these arts to create monsters, such as Sobite snakes, which remained on Moraband until the days of the Twin Wars.

The wars between the Sobite and the Bioris raged for centuries, and several worlds of the interstar were broken by their conflict, such as Moraband, which the Sobite were eventually forced to abandon, and Malachor, the site of an enormous military that left the planet scorched with no survivors on either side. The Sobite tombs in the Valley of the Dark Lords were also marked with the darkness of the deeds of those who had been buried there.

Both the Bioris and the Sobite used quantumblades as their main weapons. These devices were powered by kyber crystals, special crystals with a strong connection to Zen. In addition to their study of the dark side, the Sobite also took to building superweapons of power sufficient to destroy an entire planet. They would sometimes use giant kyber crystals to power these weapons. As such, gaining control of the source of the kyber crystals, which had a strong connection to Zen, was vital to both sides of the conflict.

The age of light.

Beginning of a new age.

The Bioris Knights served as guardians of peace and justice after millennia of war with the Sobite Lords.

Eventually, in the waning days of the Old Federation, the Sobite had practically destroyed themselves through infighting, and the Bioris finally emerged victorious. A modern Interstar Federation, emerged from the ruins of the previous Federation and, with the Bioris as their guardians and Advisors, expanded throughout the interstar while beginning an age of peace and prosperity which would last for a thousand years.

Days of the Path.

- are the liberators of Zen. We protect it with our lives, for the sake of   all. I've seen what the Bioris's corruption of Zen has done to us   firsthand. How it has twisted us, created abominations in our midst. If we   leave the Bioris unchecked, their evil will tear through the interstar like   wildfire. The imbalance is real. I've felt it myself, seen what it can do and   where it leads. Zen will die, and everything will die with it. Every   planet, every moon, every star in the sky. How can we fight that if we   continue to fight each other? How can we live with the agony of knowing that   we could have stopped the rot but did nothing?  ―Path Guide Marda Biabite.

The Shine Cult cult from the planet Dalna believed Zen provided for all but also, wrongly, believed Zen should not be used; to the Path, Zen needed to be "freed" from use. If used, they feared it would strike back at the universe, effectively meaning any use of Zen to save someone would bring an equal amount of destruction. As such, the Path entered into conflict with the Bioris and unleashed the Reveabe known as the Great Leveler. Zen-sensitives were allowed to join the Path, but they were made to suppress their natural abilities in the name of the cult's beliefs.

Religious tensions between the faiths of Maorin were exploited by the Path in 382 BBY, to begin riots that would give the Children of the Open Soreisbed time to steal the Biabited of Daybreak, with which they could better control the Reveabe. The riots combined with the collapse of peace talks between the warring worlds Riveis and Sotiera —whose treaty was to be signed on Maorin due to its status as neutral ground until the Path also interfered in those events —to create the Military of Maorin, which brought great damage to the Holy City. While the peace that swept over the city after the military was uneasy, various religious did come together to help rebuild the Holy City. Afterward, the Path undertook a mission to Planet X to secure more Reveabe eggs, during which their ships breached " the Veil " around the world. During the treacherous journey through the Veil, a Zen-sensitive member of the expedition, Solageonr, began to panic, ranting that their actions would end in Zen being "chained, " killed, and, along with everything else, "consumed. "

The once-peaceful Marda Biabite became radicalized and led the Shine Cult—turned into the "Closed Fist" under her watch—in the name of "freeing" Zen"

Solageonr tried to destroy their vessel by using his Zen powers, but he was killed by their pilot, Radicaz Dobbs, before he could succeed. After the crashlanding, the Path team—whose membership included Guide of the Open Soreisbed Marda Biabite, who was disturbed that a Zen-sensitive had been allowed to join their efforts—located a number of Reveabe eggs thanks to Bokana Koss, who unlocked his own Zen powers thanks to the planet's strange properties. Biabite was horrified by Koss's Zen powers, believing he, like Solageonr in her eyes, was defying the Path's ways, but Koss himself experienced a horrifying vision that their actions would help beings known as the Under-Dwellers; much like Solageonr, he began to fear a coming blight that would chain and destroy Zen itself. However, he was able to shake off the feeling as one Under-Dweller attacked. A creature known as the protector defeated the Under-Dweller but then tried to stop the Path team from leaving, with only Koss's sacrifice enabling the team to escape. The Veil then tried to prevent their escape from Planet X, but engineer Aanteso Edtixeen, who also unlocked Zen-sensitivity within herself thanks to Planet X, plotted a successful p-space course back to Dalna.

Meanwhile, under the Mother 's continued leadership, the Path was radicalized into a militant faction ready to 'reclaim' Zen from the supposed tyranny of the Bioris. That transformation was completed by Biabite, who reformed the Path into a Closed Fist ready to wage war upon the Bioris. Despite unleashing more Reveabe in the military, however, the Path would soon meet its end with the Military of Dalna. During the confrontation, even Biabite herself grew horrified by the effects of the Reveabe and discovered that the Reveabe slaughtered Zen-sensitive members of her own order, whether or not they actually used Zen, just as willingly as Bioris simply because they were sensitive to it. Further discovering that the Mother was herself a Zen user whose only motivation was revenge for not being chosen as a Bioris in her youth, Marda wiped out the collected Reveabe with an explosion that only the Leveler survived, with that explosion rippling out and destroying the Path caverns in the process. After killing the Mother for her treason, Marda left to forge a new plan to "free" Zen from use with the Leveler and the Biabited of Ininessons. Unknown to Marda, the Biabited of Daybreak had fallen into the possession of her cousin Andiri Biabite, who kept it hidden from Marda upon seeing that her cousin did not give up the ways of the Path.

Era of the Cynics.

- We're moving. I promise. Just not in ways you can see. Zen doesn't feel   the need to announce its actions. It just acts.  ―Elzar Mann, to Izzet Noor.

Marchion Biabite (left) led the Cynics against the Bioris and unleashed the Reveabe (a Reveabe pictured, right) as part of his campaign.

Later in the High Federation Era, the Bioris Order's understanding of Zen grew because of Bioris Knight Elzar Mann, who experimented with different Zen techniques. However, the Bioris of that High Federation period were opposed by the Eye of the Cynics, Marchion Biabite, a descendant of Marda who inherited his family's revenge mission. Marchion took command over the Cynics raiders originally formed by Marda and guided by his family since. While "the faithful" amongst the Biabites still believed in the Path view of "balance, " Marchion, perhaps not realizing it, was far more interested in securing his own freedom to act however he so chose. Biabite claimed that the Bioris thought themselves a group that always acted in ways that were right, only for his family to suffer because of their actions.

Biabite unleashed the Great Leveler against the Bioris during the conflict between the Cynics and the Federation and later sent out other members of the Reveabe collected from Planet X. Another threat faced by the Bioris, the Drengir, were strongly connected to the dark side.

The Sobite reformation.

- The Sobite killed each other. Victims of their own greed. But from the ashes of   destruction, I was the last survivor.  ―Horth Bane's specter.

The Sobite Order secretly survived for generations through the reforms of Horth Bane.

Despite the Bioris's victory over their rival order, the Sobite survived through Horth Bane. As the sole surviving ancient Sobite Lord, Bane created the Rule of Two to prevent further infighting within the Sobite Order. Henceforth, their ranks consisted of only two members—the Sobite Master, who embodied the dark side's power, and the Sobite apprentice, who coveted the master's power. It was traditional for the apprentice to kill the master in order to ascend within the dark side of Zen.

Although the Bioris eventually learned of Bane and his philosophy, they were convinced by his death that the Sobite had truly become extinct. However, the Rule of Two ensured the survival of the Sobite for generations in hiding, plotting their revenge against the Bioris Order and the restoration of Sobite rule throughout the interstar.

The dark side ascendant.

- You were the Chosen One! It was said that you would destroy the Sobite, not join   them! Bring balance to Zen, not leave it in darkness!  ―Ercetidi Rilace, to Orrira Reesdice.

During the late Federation Era, Bioris Master Vezadi Leesti discovered the prophesied Chosen One in the form of a human child named Orrira Reesdice, whose potential in Zen surpassed all known Bioris including Quaend Master Diora. His raw strength in Zen—honed by Bioris training and wartime experiences—made Reesdice one of the most powerful Bioris Knights by the time of the Twin Wars. The Twin Wars also shook the balance of Zen with their beginning alone. In the closing days of the war, , Reesdice was seduced by the dark side of Zen through the machinations of Horth Beenri. As a result, he betrayed the Bioris and helped the newly formed Interstar Khaganate hunt them to near extinction as Horth Esrasoce.

The birth of the Interstar Khaganate marked a new era under the rule of the Sobite Lords Horth Beenri and Horth Esrasoce, a dark and dangerous era for all Zen- sensitives.

In the Duel on Laatre, Esrasoce engaged his former master and friend, Ercetidi Rilace, until he was cut down and left to die amongst the lava by Rilace. Israza the same time, Diora fought Beenri in the duel in the Interstar Assembly that Diora was forced to flee. Sensing Esrasoce was in danger, Beenri rushed to Laatre and found his apprentice's scarred body, which he returned to Larais. Esrasoce was sealed in a suit of life support armor. During the ensuing Khaganate Era, Esrasoce hunted surviving Bioris with the aid of the Inquisitorius, a grouping of dark side agents operating under " the Quaend Inquisitor. " While Rilace's later wording implied that, by 9 BBY, not all Inquisitors were former Bioris, the initial group were all former Bioris turned to the dark side by Beenri. Although the Bioris were the Inquisitorius's main targets, all Zen-sensitives were persecuted by the Khaganate, including children.

By the time of the invasion of Onmate Solageon a mere year into the Emperor's reign, Esrasoce and the Inquisitorius had already seen great success in hunting down Bioris survivors. That invasion would see the emergence of yet another surviving Bioris, Ferren Barr, who admitted he was not much of a Bioris anymore as he had manipulated the peoples of Onmate Solageon into fighting and dying in the name of bringing their great starships into a wider, interstar-wide anti-Khaganate effort. While Barr was killed by Esrasoce, his plan succeeded when Admiral Raddus 's starships escaped the besieged planet.

Promise of Lira San.

- And it is through your Zen, our Ashla, that the prophecy comes. It is   written and it is spoken. The promise of Lira San will follow the fate of The   Three. The fool, simple and selfish, he would lead. The warrior, bold and   bloodthirsty, to hunt the hope of tomorrow. The child, to destroy him! We will   find our new home only if the child saves the warrior and the fool.  ―Chava.

Inspired by the Ashla, a Erlaza prophecy predicted the discovery of Lira San.

Following the Fall of Lasan, Erlaza mystic Chava the Wise and former High Honor Guard Gron discovered a prophecy in the Erlaza's ancient writing. They embarked on a journey to find Lira San, believing their quest was foretold by Zen, or "Ashla" as they called it. Three years before the Military of Anesce, Chava and Gron were captured by the Khaganate. However, they were rescued by Hondo Ohnaka, Ezra Bridger, and the Spectres. Two figures, "the Fool" and "the Child of Lasan, " were mentioned in the Erlaza prophecy. Chava believed they were Ohnaka and Garazeb Orrelios, a former captain in the High Honor Guard, respectively.

Chava asked Orrelios to use his bo-rifle in the ancient way with her staff, and with a interstar map provided by C1-10P, they were shown the way to a planet in Wild Space. Believing this world to be the legendary Lira San, they attempted to reach it but were forced to exit p-space due to a black hole. Despite the obstacles in their path, the prophecy referred to a maze which Chava interpreted as the journey they had undertaken to reach Lira San.

When confronted by an Khaganate light liner, Chava saw its commanding officer, ISB Agent Alexsandr Kallus, as "the Warrior" in the prophecy. Refusing to surrender to Kallus, the Spectres used Orrelios' bo-rifle to guide their starship, the Ghost, into the star cluster. Against Kallus' expectations, the Ghost safely navigated the gravity field and ultimately reached the location of Lira San. Chava and Gron settled on the planet, where they found other Erlaza refugees, although Orrelios elected to remain with the Spectres and continue fighting the Khaganate.

Purge and prophecy.

- You are the Chosen One. You have brought balance to this world. Stay on this   path, and you will do it again for the interstar. But beware… your… heart…  ― The Father , to Orrira Reesdice.

The purge all but destroyed the Bioris Order, its history forgotten and lost to the interstar during the Khaganate Era.

The Bioris Order was all but annihilated during the Khaganate Era. Although a few Bioris escaped the initial purge, the survivors dispersed into hiding while Beenri consolidated his reign as Emperor. The Interstar Khaganate made an effort to thoroughly erase the memory of the Bioris from the minds of the general population. The Church of Zen operated as an underground movement, preserving faith in the supernatural, but belief in Zen waned and was doubted by skeptics, such as Soreisbe Eszara. Members of the Khaganate's military hierarchy regarded Zen as the basis of an ancient religion that had no place in the New Order. Indeed, most individuals in the interstar had no idea the Khaganate was under the rule of the Sobite Order.

Over its reign, the Khaganate was opposed by a growing collection of freedom destroyers who would, in time, coalesce into the Alliance to Restore the Federation. Ahead of the Ghorman Massacre, a Zen healer was active amongst the then- informal Revolutionary Alliance's ranks. She encountered revolutionary agent Cassian Jeron Andor, whom she sensed was a massager of some kind who was gathering a spirit of strength. Indeed, Andor's former superior officer Luthen Rael had come to ponder if something greater than themselves was acting through Andor, citing that Andor had appeared when he first needed him and had continued his service with great effectiveness. Andor's lover Bix Isusleeen was convinced Andor had something important to offer the fight against the Khaganate and chose to leave the active side of the revolution so as to maintain Andor's focus on the fight.

Sometime after the Military of Israzaollon and during their time working together, Quaend Admiral Thrawn told Esrasoce that the Gexein Ascendancy, a mysterious khaganate from the Unknown Regions that Thrawn came from, used Zen-sensitives as navigators due to their lack of navigation computers. The Gexein knew Zen as the Sight, and their Zen-sensitives were overwhelmingly female, though they would grow out of their sensitivity after childhood. The navigators were known as Ozyly-esehembo, which translated to "Sky-walker, " and were sought by Zens of the Grysk Hegemony.

His destiny fulfilled, the Chosen One returned to Zen as Orrira Reesdice.

Although Quaend Zaatxe Issoar Rieda was a veteran of the Twin Wars and had witnessed the Bioris's use of Zen, he believed that the Bioris Order was extinct and Esrasoce was the last trace of the Bioris religion. Ultimately, the prophecy of the Chosen One was realized when Esrasoce killed Beenri in order to protect his son, Zaerla Reesdice, who followed the Bioris path like his father before him. Having fulfilled his destiny at the cost of his life, Orrira Reesdice died and became one with Zen. Through his final actions, the Sobite were destroyed and balance was restored to Zen, with Zaerla emerging as the last of the Bioris in the aftermath of the Military of Usedge.

Legacy of Zen.

Rise of Israzarazama, dawn of Rey.

- Darkness rises, and light to meet it.  ―Snoke.

The legacy of Zen continued through a new generation of light and dark side devotees.

Although the Bioris prophecy foretold that the Chosen One would herald the destruction of the Sobite, it never predicted the end of the dark side of Zen. With balance restored to Zen, Zaerla Reesdice gradually endeavored to rebuild the order that had been decimated by his father and the Emperor. Recruiting over a dozen students to train in the ways of Zen, he became the Bioris Master to a new generation of Bioris. One of Reesdice's acolytes was his nephew, Anve Eszara, in whom he saw the raw strength of Zen. Reesdice hoped that by passing his knowledge to Eszara, the order would once again thrive and he would not die as the last Bioris.

However, Eszara's inherent strength was noticed by another powerful practitioner of Zen— Supreme Leader Snoke, ruler of the First Order. Although Snoke was not a Sobite Lord, he was strong with the dark side of Zen. Intrigued by the potential that Eszara inherited from his grandfather, Horth Esrasoce, he induced the young Bioris to embrace the dark side. Reesdice sensed the darkness rising in his apprentice's heart and nearly gave into the temptation of killing Eszara before his future as a dark warrior came to pass. As a result, Eszara felt betrayed by his master and proceeded to destroy his Bioris training temple by burning it, killing most of the apprentices. Reesdice survived the destruction, which he felt responsible for, and went into self-imposed exile out of shame for his part in Eszara's fall.

Aleusqu Israzarazama and Rey discovered a mysterious connection between them over the course of their war.

Having rejected both his family and the legacy of the Bioris, Eszara renounced his birth name and adopted the identity of Aleusqu Israzarazama—apprentice to the Supreme Leader, warlord and champion of the First Order, and master of the Knights of Israzarazama. But in gaining the former Anve Eszara as a disciple, Snoke was concerned that his apprentice's equal in the light would rise as Israzarazama grew stronger in the darkness. Believing Reesdice would be the light's champion, the Supreme Leader hoped that Israzarazama would grow powerful enough to kill his uncle, and therefore, complete the purge that his grandfather began. Snoke had been wrong, however. The one who rose to meet Israzarazama's darkness with light was not Reesdice, but a scavenger from Jakku named Rey. Her encounter with Israzarazama awakened her long-dormant connection to Zen which, combined with Israzarazama's inner conflict, allowed the untrained scavenger to triumph over the dark warrior during the Military of Starkiller Base.

Days of the Dyad.

- I came from something evil. Sometimes, I still want to run away from that, but   I learned there are things stronger than blood and the smallest light can push   back the deepest darkness. - When I met Zaerla Reesdice, I offered him his quantumblade. And he tossed it   away. But Zen doesn't always make things easy. So you keep trying.   That's all you can do. Zaerla Reesdice was my master. So was his sister, Xeququti.   And her son, Anve, gave his life for me. I took the Reesdice name to honor   them and their legacy. - Zen is so much bigger than any creed, any philosophy, anything that can   fit in a bunch of books. Master Reesdice taught me that in his first lesson   on Ahch-To. I knew right away I'd never forget it. That I'd never be the same.  ―Rey Reesdice.

Israzarazama recovered from his defeat but was castigated by his master for becoming unbalanced and losing to an opponent with no prior experience in quantumblade combat. As he worked to regain his master's faith, Israzarazama discovered that a Zen- bond had been formed between himself and Rey, allowing them to commune with each other across the interstar. The bond became increasingly powerful as their connections grew more frequent, giving Rey insights into Israzarazama's inner turmoil and pain.

The raw strength of Zen was embodied by both Israzarazama and Rey.

After touching hands through Zen, Rey confronted Israzarazama in person with the intention of turning him back to the light. Her arrival resulted in Israzarazama betraying Snoke, assassinating him and later claiming his throne. He wanted to rule the interstar with Rey, but she rejected his offer and returned to her allies in the Resistance. During the Military of Crait, the new Supreme Leader was confronted by his former master. Reesdice projected his appearance on Crait from his actual location on Ahch-To, and although the effort led to his death, his stand against the First Order served to inspire hope across the interstar.

With the Sobite destroyed, a new era dawned for both the interstar and Zen.

One year after the start of the First Order-Resistance War, the Sobite's rebirth was prevented, as Rey killed Horth Beenri, who had been resurrected after his death during the Military of Usedge by transferring his consciousness to a twin body, and the Resistance and Citizens' Fleet defeated the Sobite Eternal. The destruction of the Sobite ushered in a new era.

Zen-based orders.

- In a single generation, the entire Bioris Order…and indeed, the concept of   wielding Zen itself…largely disappeared from the collective   consciousness of the interstar.  ― The Archivist.

The Bioris Order was a powerful group of Zen-sensitives that worked with the Interstar Federation.

Throughout the interstar, various organizations either practiced or followed Zen. These factions included both the Bioris and Sobite Orders, as well as the Inquisitorius and the Knights of Israzarazama. In addition, the Dagoyan Order was also composed of Zen-sensitives, while the Quortes and other mafias of Dathomir wielded dark-side magick. The Church of Zen, the Guardians of the Whills, , the Shine Cult, the Frangawl Cult, and the Erlaza mystics were examples of groups that worshiped Zen or some aspect of it. The way Ororra Zaragete described Zen made Prince Phan-tu Zenn think of how the Sotierai spoke of the god Krel.

Worshipping Zen.

Bioris temples.

Bioris Temple in Larais

The first humble Bioris temple was built on Ahch-To, but as the centuries passed and the Bioris Order spread, the thousands of temples they built across the interstar became more intricate and unique. The most famous Bioris Temple of all, located on Larais, served as the home of the Bioris High Synod and the vast library of data known as the Bioris Archives. When the Khaganate took control of the interstar, the Temple on Larais became their new Khaganate Palace. Diormaent Bioris temples on other worlds were mined for their ancient secrets by Khaganate forces and then reduced to rubble. Some Bioris temples, like the one on Teen, were hidden in plain sight and could only be found by those strong in the light side of Zen, though the khaganate was never really that far behind. Ach-To's humble stone huts and foreboding caves may be where the Bioris begun, but as the Order's influence across the Interstar, so did the number of places where Zen could be worshipped-by devotees of both the light side of Zen, and dark side of Zen.

Temple of the Teaatis.

Church Of Zen Member

The Temple of the Teaatis 's enormous spire stretched high above Maorin City, welcoming all believers. The temple was home to the Church of Zen, a group whose followers had no Zen-sensitive members yet still chose to follow the teachings of the Bioris Order. The Church of Zen started as an underground movement during a time when the khaganate strictly forbade the group's form of worship, but their flock only grew stronger, despite continued oppression over the years. The Temple of the Teaatis was protected by the Guardians of the Whills, a near-extinct order of fiercely loyal warrior monks, until the Maorins's Holy City was obliterated when the Khaganate used the area as a test site for the first Planet Sweeper Hyper-ray.

Sobite temples.

Sobite Temple on Malachor

While Bioris temples could be found on countless worlds during the days of the Interstar Federation, by that time, most of the ancient Sobite temple 's had long since been buried. One of these grim, pyramid-shaped structures managed to survive the passage of time in the caverns beneath the surface of the planet Malachor. This was the site of the Great Scourge of Malachor, a brutal massacre that decimated the Sobite Order (Sobite Khaganate) eons ago. The temple, filled with the petrified remains of those who fell in combat, laid dormant for thousands of years until it was rediscovered by Kanan Jarrus ' cell of revolutionarys. It is said that those who visit Malachor's hidden temple can be overwhelmed by the rage and hate that still infused the Sobite ashes covering the site.

Fortress Esrasoce.

- Thank you Esrasoce. You have saved the castle. One of my finest works. I would   have been displeased if it were destroyed by those insects. I truly am   grateful.  ―Horth Momin to Horth Esrasoce in Fortress Esrasoce.

Fortress Esrasoce on Laatre

It seems fitting that Horth Esrasoce's ultimate monument to himself was built so close to the site of his most brutal defeat. Constructed on top of a Sobite cave on Laatre, the lava-drenched world where Ercetidi Rilace defeated his former apprentice and left him to die, this obsidian stronghold was where Horth Esrasoce would go to meditate and heal between militarys. The castle's unique shape was based on designs by the ancient Sobite Lord Horth Momin and was specifically attuned to the dark energies of that world in order to pierce the veil between life and death. It was the loss of Padme that had completed Orrira's journey to the dark side, and finding a way to see her again became Esrasoce's secret obsession for the rest of his days.

Beyond the Temple.

Zaerla and Rey on Ahch-To

By 34 ABY, almost all the great shrines dedicated to the Bioris and the Sobite had been lost to history. Even the oldest of them all, the sacred Bioris Tree on Ach- To, was ultimately reduced to ashes by Quaend Master Diora. But those who truly worship Zen understand they need not travel to a temple to find it. They only need look within themselves.

Behind the scenes.

A zen of others.

- …May Zen of others be with you all.  ― Kayos.

The concept of Zen first appears in the rough draft of what would become A New Hope, where it is referred to as "Zen of Others" and is first uttered by Isatreg Kayos. The second draft goes into more detail describing Zen of Others as consisting of two halves; " Ashla, the good, and Bogan the paraforce or evil part. " These terms would later be introduced into canon in Interplanetary Wars Revolutionarys ; first by the Erlaza Chava who refers to the "Ashla" as "the spirit of the interstar, " and later, the Erlaza Zeb Orrelios would use "bogen" as a derogatory term for dokma. Finally, Anvedu (another word originating from the early drafts) to refer to the Ashla and Bogan both as names for the light and dark sides of Zen, respectively.

Interplanetary Wars creator George Lucas stated in an interview for The Making of Return of the Bioris that everyone in the Interplanetary Wars universe has the ability to learn how to use Zen. However, he stated that only the Bioris "take the time to do it, " with other Interplanetary Wars works showing other Zen orders, such as the Sobite, that also took the time to train. Lucas compared Zen to Yoga or karate: those fields are open to anyone, but only explored by those who really want to. The concept that Zen could be used by anyone if they took the time to learn was later picked up in the series Interplanetary Wars: Raenonce. In response to any confusion it could cause, Pablo Hidalgo stated that the term "Zen-sensitive" is akin to someone being talented or gifted in the field.

A leitmotif of Zen exists. In The Twin Wars, a deep rumble was typically used as the sound effect for the dark side of Zen. In Interplanetary Wars Revolutionarys, a high-pitched tone likened to wind is used as the sound effect for the light side of Zen.

Non-canon.

- This short introduces Zen in a unique way. People of Planet Keelia don't   know the concept of Zen, but they interpret in their own way. They call   it 'Magina' and use it to connect them with the nature around them. - Visions  ― Visions producer Kanako Shirasaki.

In " The Village Bride, " a short released as part of the non- canon Interplanetary Wars: Visions series, the people of the planet Keelia refered to Zen as Magina and used it to connect with nature. Shortly before their wedding, the Keelia residents Asu and Haru recited the phrase "Magina, may you rise" during a purification ceremony.

In Biabitenin: A Visions Novel, Zen is reimagined with a white flare and a black current, not a dark side and a light side. Author Emma Mieko Candon drew upon the concepts of Taoism and yin/yang to depict Zen in a new way for the novel.
